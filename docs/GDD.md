# Roaring Lions — Game Design Document

**Version:** 1.1 · **Status:** decisions locked; §5 synced to the calibrated M0 model
**Companion docs:** `CLAUDE.md` (working conventions), `docs/ART_PIPELINE.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`

---

## 1. Overview

**Roaring Lions** is an open-source, single-player real-time strategy game in the Command & Conquer tradition, played in 2:1 dimetric projection. The player commands the 401st "Ari'im" Brigade of the **Kedem Defense Forces** through a campaign of urban and rural operations across the fictional **Sahar Basin**.

The game's distinguishing claim is that its combat model **simulates real engagement odds** rather than trading hit points. Fights are resolved through a detect → hit → penetrate → component-damage chain, with suppression as the dominant battlefield force. Firepower is abundant; the scarce resources are information, initiative, and restraint.

### Design pillars

1. **Information is the real resource.** What you know beats what you own. Recon quality propagates forward into every later phase.
2. **Restraint is a mechanic, not a lecture.** Rules-of-engagement scoring gates progression. Precision under constraint is more interesting than unlimited firepower.
3. **Asymmetry is structural.** The player is few, expensive, high-tech, and visible. The enemy is cheap, numerous, and hidden. Neither side plays the same game.
4. **Everything is data.** Units, buildings, missions, and effects are JSON. The engine is code; the game is content.

## 2. Setting

All geography, factions, and place names are **fictional**. Enemy forces are defined exclusively by **military doctrine** — never by ethnicity, nationality, or faith. This is both a design decision (doctrine is more mechanically legible) and a practical one (real-world depictions have historically caused RTS titles to be banned in major markets).

**Player nation:** Kedem. **Player force:** Kedem Defense Forces (KDF), 401st "Ari'im" Brigade — the Roaring Lions.

**Region:** the Sahar Basin. Three borders, three doctrines:

| Region | Terrain | Faction | Doctrine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Marj Strip** | dense coastal urban enclave | Ashwar Front | tunnels, IEDs, ambush, human terrain |
| **Sur** | northern mountains | Sarim Brigades | rockets, ATGMs, best-trained, standoff |
| **Naharin** | eastern river desert | Rif Cells | technicals, raids, smuggling, mobility |

**Towns:** Beit Sahwan, Khan Rafid, Deir Amun (Marj) · Tel Marum, Umm Zeitoun (Sur) · Wadi Halam (Naharin).

**Layout.** Kedem sits in the centre of the basin with all three fronts on its borders,
and the geography sets the order of the war: **proximity, then standoff, then source.**

- **The Marj Strip — west, coastal.** Pressed between the sea and Kedem's most populous
  coastal plain, with no mountain, no river and no depth between them. An attack out of
  the Marj is inside Kedem's cities in minutes, which is why the war opens here and why
  it opens with a perimeter being lost rather than a push.
- **Sur — north, mountains.** Rockets range onto Kedem's north from behind a mountain
  wall. Second by sequencing rather than choice: you cannot climb into mountains with
  something at your throat on the coast.
- **Naharin — east, river desert.** The smuggling corridor that supplied the Marj's
  tunnels and Sur's rocket stocks. Last, because cutting supply is only decisive once the
  fronts it feeds are contained.

Every region is defined by terrain and doctrine, never by a people — see `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## 3. Core loop

**Minute to minute:** position for observation → acquire contacts → decide engage/bypass/mark → commit force → manage suppression and casualties → consolidate ground.

**Mission to mission:** spend the campaign ledger you earned → accept or refuse ROE risk → carry survivors and intel forward.

### Economy — two resources

**Logistics** — arrives by convoy and airdrop from a supply line the enemy can interdict. Builds units and structures. Constrained by throughput, not by a harvester loop; protecting the corridor is the gameplay.

**Intel** — generated by drones, scout teams, informants, and SIGINT structures. Spends on airstrikes, satellite sweeps, precision strikes, and revealing subterranean networks.

The split is the point. Logistics buys mass; Intel buys certainty. Most missions cannot afford both.

### Economy anchors

The cost curve prices units against each other; these anchors tie it to time. All provisional until mission playtests.

- Logistics income: Foothold ~120/min with the corridor intact, Build-up ~200/min, Clearance ~80/min trickle. Interdiction halves it.
- A Clearance mission's total spend (starting force included) ≈ one combined-arms company: 2 MBT, 2 IFV, 4–6 squads, support — ~4,500–5,500 logistics.
- Intel: drone loiter ~8/min, scout team in position ~5/min, SIGINT structure ~12/min. Satellite sweep 150 · precision strike 250 · reveal subterranean 200.
- Sanity rule: banking >30% unspent at a win means income is too high or objectives too cheap.

## 4. Phase system

Six phases. Each is normally the spine of its own **short mission** (see §6), and each writes to the campaign ledger that later phases read.

1. **Breach** — the enemy attacks and the player is the one holding. Outnumbered and converged on from every approach, holding a walled perimeter with the road still open behind it: survive, get the families inside the wire, and still hold the ground when relief arrives. The corridor is what separates a player who spends from one who does nothing — logistics accrues, and banking it is the losing move. Losing ground is the design, not a failure state. Any doctrine can open on the back foot.
2. **Recon** — drones, scout teams, snipers. Mark tunnel mouths, weapon caches, civilian-occupied structures. Low force, high stealth, heavy Intel generation.
3. **Foothold** — establish the FOB under harassment. Engineering, supply corridor, Iron Dome battery. Defensive, time-pressured.
4. **Build-up** — production and force composition. The one phase where the player has breathing room; Intel is spent here on the coming assault.
5. **Clearance** — block-by-block combined arms. The main combat phase. ROE pressure peaks.
6. **Subterranean** — escort the EOD squad to shafts and the command bunker while the enemy commits everything remaining.

### Carry-over is the system

Phases are not independent set-pieces. Recon quality propagates:

- Thorough recon → tunnel mouths pre-marked, civilian zones flagged, ambush sites known before Phase 6
- Rushed recon → shafts found under fire, ROE penalties stacking, casualties from unmarked IEDs

This is one system expressed six ways, not six minigames.

## 5. Combat model

The heart of the project. All values are Q16.16 fixed-point; all randomness draws from a seeded per-entity PRNG.

The formulas in this section are reference implementations. The §5.7 targets are the authority: where calibration forces a deviation, the deviation is correct, lives in `packages/sim/src/tuning.ts` with its reasoning, and this document follows it. Arguing formula text against a passing backtest is backwards.

### 5.1 Detection

Evaluated per observer-target pair per tick.

```
signature       = base_sig × motion_mult × firing_mult × cover_mult
strength        = optics × signature / (range² × occlusion)
P(detect, dt)   = 1 − exp(−k · strength · dt)
```

Contact confidence accumulates 0→1 across three states: **unknown → suspected (blip, no ID) → identified**. Firing multiplies signature sharply — shooting reveals you, which is what makes the asymmetry real rather than cosmetic.

### 5.2 Hit

```
P(hit) = base_accuracy
       × range_falloff(r)              // exp(−k_class·(r / r_eff)²)
       × cover_mod                     // 0.2 … 1.0
       × target_motion_mod
       × shooter_stance_mod            // stationary / short-halt / moving
       × suppression_mod(shooter)      // 1 / (1 + k·S)
```

`range_falloff` is per weapon class: guided weapons (ATGM) use k ≈ 0.25 — inside the envelope, accuracy is launch-condition-dominated, not range-dominated. This is what makes ATGM Pk ≈ 0.7 real rather than aspirational.

### 5.3 Penetration

A probability curve, not a threshold. This single change is most of what separates a simulation from a hit-point exchange.

```
effective_armor = armor[facing] × obliquity_factor × ERA_state × APS_state
P(pen)          = Φ((penetration − effective_armor) / σ)
σ               ≈ 0.12 × penetration        // dispersion
```

Facing is derived from the angle between shot vector and hull forward: **front ±60°, rear ±60°, side otherwise**. Flanking is therefore an emergent tactic rather than a scripted bonus.

### 5.4 Component damage

On penetration, roll a weighted component table (weights shift with penetration overmatch):

| Result | Base weight | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Crew shaken | 45% | large suppression spike, no material damage |
| Mobility kill | 20% | immobilised, weapons live |
| Firepower kill | 15% | mobile, cannot engage |
| Mobility + firepower | 12% | combat-ineffective, crew alive |
| Catastrophic | 8% | destroyed, crew lost |

Note that most penetrations do **not** destroy the vehicle. Recoverable losses make the battlefield legible and make casualty-averse play meaningful.

### 5.5 Suppression — the highest-value mechanic in the game

Real firefights are overwhelmingly suppressive fire. Modelling this does more for how combat *feels* than any other single system.

```
S += near_miss_weight × caliber_factor      // accumulates from near misses
S *= exp(−λ·dt)                             // λ ≈ 0.15 / s
accuracy_mult = 1 / (1 + k·S)               // k ≈ 1.5
S > 0.7  → unit goes to ground: halts and holds fire (see 5.5a)
```

Suppression makes machine guns and mortars valuable without them killing anything, which is exactly correct and almost never modelled.

An earlier draft had going to ground merely halve movement — calibration showed that lets assaults creep through defensive fire and flattens the 3:1 curve. Fire superiority only means something if it silences a side.

### 5.5a Breaking — rout and surrender

Soft units pinned continuously for ~10 s **rout**: they abandon orders and flee toward cover away from fire. Routed units approached rather than shot **surrender** and become prisoners. Prisoners score ROE credit where kills score nothing — restraint must be the mechanically superior play, not the sentimental one. Vehicles have the component table; this is the infantry equivalent, and it is why firefights end in collapses instead of mutual extermination. (Rout lands in M1; surrender and prisoner handling in M2.)

### 5.6 Protective systems

**Trophy APS** (vehicle): ablative interception, `P(intercept) = base_pk × f(threat_velocity) × (1 − saturation_penalty)`. Effective against ATGM/RPG (0.6–0.9), near-useless against APFSDS. Finite magazine with reload delay — **saturation attacks are the counter**, which gives the enemy a real doctrine.

**Iron Dome** (structure): intercepts ballistic rocket fire in an arc. Pk ≈ 0.85 per interceptor. Interceptors cost Logistics, so the player chooses what to protect.

### 5.7 Validation targets

The model is wrong unless the backtest harness reproduces these:

- Urban assault requires roughly **3:1** attacker:defender for reliable success
- ATGM Pk vs unprotected armour ≈ **0.7**
- APS intercept rate **0.6–0.9** vs shaped charge
- **Lanchester's square law emerges naturally** — concentration of force wins superlinearly. If it doesn't, the model is broken regardless of how good the numbers look individually.

All four pass as of M0 (`pnpm balance`). Extended targets, landing as the mechanics arrive (M1–M2), so the harness validates the enemy doctrines and not only the player's toolkit:

- **Ambush (Ashwar):** an unspotted RPG team in `ambush(3)` gets its first volley off before being identified in ≥80% of engagements against a non-reconnoitred column.
- **Standoff (Sarim):** an ATGM cell firing from ≥8 tiles wins the cost exchange against an APS-equipped MBT over a 3-missile engagement.
- **Raid (Rif):** technicals that engage and disengage against a slower force escape with ≥60% survival; technicals that get caught die fast. Mobility must actually be their armour.

### 5.8 Legibility — the model is shown, not hidden

A probabilistic model the player cannot read is indistinguishable from bad RNG. The ROE rule — you always know what a shot will cost before taking it — applies to the whole combat model:

- Hovering a target shows projected P(hit) with its dominant factors (range, cover, motion, suppression).
- Selected units show effective-range rings; suppression and pinned states are always visible on the unit plate.
- The debug overlay is the developer form of this principle, not a substitute for it.

If a fight feels broken, the UI has failed before the model has.

## 6. Missions

**Unit of content: one short mission, 5–7 minutes.** Three to five missions per town. Each mission is led by one phase.

This is deliberate. A 45-minute five-phase mission makes balance iteration prohibitive — nobody re-plays 40 minutes to verify a tuning change, least of all a volunteer. Short missions are testable in isolation and authorable by a contributor who does not understand the whole campaign.

### The campaign ledger

Carry-over persists between missions rather than within one. Missions declare a **contract**: what ledger keys they require on entry, what they produce on exit.

```json
{
  "intel.tunnel_mouths_marked": ["tm_04", "tm_07"],
  "intel.civilian_zones_flagged": ["cz_01", "cz_02", "cz_09"],
  "roster.surviving_units": [{ "id": "u_112", "type": "mbt_lavi", "veterancy": 2 }],
  "roe.cumulative_rating": 78
}
```

Beit Sahwan III reads `tunnel_mouths_marked`. If Mission I went badly the list is short and the player finds shafts under fire. This also creates free replay value: replay Mission I for a better ledger.

### Objective vocabulary

Missions are declarative trigger/objective JSON, never scripted code. Available objective types: `locate`, `mark`, `collapse`, `escort`, `hold_for`, `evacuate_before`, `eliminate_hvt`, `capture`, `survive_until`, `no_collateral_above`.

"Destroy all enemies" is available and should be used rarely.

### Behavior vocabulary

Missions stay declarative, so behaviour must be declarative too. A mission's garrison entries may declare:

- **stance** — `hold_position` (default) · `ambush(tiles)`: no fire and minimum signature until a target closes to N tiles · `patrol(waypoints)` · `garrison(structure)`
- **triggers** — `on: first_contact | casualties_pct(n) | timer_s(n) | zone_entered(id)` → `do: commit(group) | withdraw_to(point) | reinforce(group)`

One stance per unit and a handful of triggers per mission is the budget. If a mission needs behaviour this vocabulary cannot express, extend the vocabulary — never script the mission.

`ambush` is Ashwar doctrine made mechanical: an unspotted RPG team holding fire until 3 tiles is the entire reason recon quality matters by Phase 5.

### ROE scoring

Every mission scores 0–100 on rules of engagement. Civilian casualties, damage to flagged structures, and ordnance disproportionate to target all deduct. The rating gates unit unlocks and feeds `roe.cumulative_rating`.

This is the mechanic that makes the game a strategy title rather than a shooting gallery. It must be legible in the UI at all times — the player should always know what a shot will cost them before taking it.

## 7. Technical architecture

**Stack:** TypeScript + PixiJS. pnpm workspaces monorepo.

| Package | Responsibility | May import |
|---|---|---|
| `@lions/sim` | deterministic simulation | nothing |
| `@lions/render` | Pixi rendering, VFX | `@lions/sim` (read-only types) |
| `@lions/data` | content JSON + schemas | nothing |
| `@lions/app` | shell, input, UI, campaign | all |
| `tools/` | render rig, validators, balance sim | — |

### Non-negotiable invariants

1. **20 Hz fixed sim tick.** Renderer interpolates to 60 fps.
2. **Q16.16 fixed-point in the sim. No floats.** IEEE-754 basic ops are bit-identical across platforms but `Math.sin/cos/pow/exp` are *not* — implementations differ between JS engines. The sim uses fixed-point arithmetic and lookup-table trigonometry, enforced by a lint rule banning `Math.*` and `Date.*` inside `@lions/sim`.
3. **All randomness from a seeded per-entity PRNG.** Never a global stream — per-entity streams keep determinism stable when entity counts change.
4. **Sim takes commands in, emits state + events out. One direction only.** VFX subscribe to events and never write back. This preserves replays and keeps future multiplayer nearly free.

### Pathfinding

Flow fields, not per-unit A*. Per-unit A* collapses above roughly 50 units and this game routinely exceeds that. One field per destination group, shared by all members, recomputed on terrain change (D9 bulldozer, structure collapse).

### Performance budget

Target 300–600 simultaneous units at 60 fps. Struct-of-arrays layout over typed arrays in the sim; no per-entity object allocation in the hot loop. If the ceiling is hit, the flow-field pass moves to WASM — the sim's isolation makes that a contained change.

## 8. Milestones

**M0 — Sandbox.** No missions, no campaign. Two forces on terrain, full combat resolution, and a debug overlay exposing every detect/hit/penetration/suppression roll. This is where the combat model is proven and it is the most important build in the project. Do not proceed until the §5.7 validation targets pass.

**M1 — Beit Sahwan.** Three short missions, campaign ledger, ROE scoring. First public playable. Open source has a hard rule: nobody contributes to a repo they cannot play. M1 exists to put a link in the README.

**M2 — v1.0.** Three towns, one per doctrine — roughly 10–14 missions. Three doctrines are required because tuning against tunnels alone overfits the model; ATGM standoff and technical raids surface failures in systems that looked settled. It also gives contributors three templates to pattern-match instead of one.

## 9. Open-source governance

**Licensing:** code MIT · art and data CC BY-SA 4.0 · all contributions under DCO sign-off · source `.blend` files required alongside rendered sprites.

**Balance protection** — the real risk in an open contribution model is a well-meaning overpowered unit:

- **Cost-curve validator.** Every unit's stats produce a power score; CI rejects submissions whose cost falls outside the tolerance band. Effectively an arbitrage bound on unit pricing.
- **Headless balance sim.** New units run 10,000 battles against a benchmark roster; win rates post as a PR comment. Reviewers see evidence, not opinion.

**AI-generated art policy:** permitted, including for shipped assets. **Disclosure is required in the PR description** wherever generative tools were used. Everything still passes the same gates as any other art — palette, binary alpha, silhouette, and the redistribution-rights requirement in ART_PIPELINE §7 — so the standard is the output, not the tool. The reasoning lives in `CONTRIBUTING.md` alongside the rule, because stated reasoning survives contributor turnover in a way a bare policy line does not.

## 10. Deferred

Explicitly out of scope for v1.0, listed so they are not accidentally designed for: multiplayer, map editor UI, naval units, air superiority layer, dynamic campaign, mod loader beyond JSON content, localisation beyond English and Hebrew.

The sim's determinism and command-based input mean multiplayer remains cheap to add later. That is the only deferred item the architecture actively protects.
