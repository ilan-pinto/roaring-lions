# Roaring Lions

A source-available real-time strategy game in the Command & Conquer tradition — 2:1 dimetric, TypeScript + three.js, a lit renderer drawing rigged 3D meshes (PixiJS remains as an unlit, sprite-only fallback via `?renderer=pixi`, slated for retirement) — whose distinguishing claim is that combat **simulates real engagement odds** instead of trading hit points. Fights resolve through a detect → hit → penetrate → component-damage chain, and suppression, not damage, is the dominant battlefield force. Single-player today; the sim is deterministic and command-driven so that multiplayer can follow.

All geography and factions are fictional. Enemy forces are defined by military doctrine — tunnels and ambush, standoff fires, mobile raiding — never by ethnicity, nationality, or faith.

## Play it

**<https://roaring-lions.pint12.workers.dev>** — the current build, served by a Cloudflare Worker built from `main`. Source and issues: <https://github.com/ilan-pinto/roaring-lions>.

The menu leads with where the campaign stands — "Start — <mission>" for a fresh player, "Continue — <mission>" once something has been played, gone once every mission is done — then Campaign and Brigade (and the tutorial by name until it is completed). Below that: replay the tutorial, free play on any map, Saves, Settings, New campaign (confirmed first; the brigade survives), the music/sound toggle, and Credits. The tutorial, *Beit Sahwan 0 — Working Up*, teaches the roster and control groups in 14 steps. Free play walks any shipped map with a full task force and optional extras — civilians with a refuge to shepherd them to, a pre-dug tunnel with two sapper squads to collapse it, the Sarim roster, an anti-tank ditch, no-fire ground — plus three diagnostic toggles (billboards instead of meshes, a showcase of every ground mark, a pre-kitted force).

It needs a WebGL2-capable browser; the Pixi fallback is a manual switch, not an automatic one.

The first minute:

- Left-drag box-selects your units; a click that stays put selects the one unit under the cursor, and a click on empty ground clears the selection. A right-click on the ground is an attack-move — units engage what they meet on the way; there is no separate plain move from the mouse. `Shift` queues the order behind the current one.
- Right-click a building and the troops that can enter it garrison it, while a demolisher — Combat Engineers or the D9 — levels it. Ordering an attack on a protected building (a hall) is refused unless `Alt` is held — the game will not spend your Conduct on an ambiguous click, and a note says why. A selection that is nothing but demolishers levels a protected building with no key needed.
- `Ctrl`+`1`–`9` (`Cmd` on a Mac) assigns a control group, `1`–`9` recalls it, and a second tap within 400 ms centres the camera on it. `Ctrl`+`A` (`Cmd`+`A`) selects every unit; `Tab` cycles the HUD chips.
- `h` halts, `f` lays smoke at the cursor, `g`/`u` load and unload, `i` selects the next idle unit, `Space` jumps to the latest alert, `b` focuses the reinforcements dock, `m` mutes. `W`/`S`/`A`/`D` and the arrows pan; the wheel zooms; the minimap takes right-click orders too. `F1` lists every binding, `Esc` backs out of whatever is open or armed and otherwise pauses, and every binding except `Tab` and `Esc` can be rebound under Settings › Controls; the control-group digits, the arrow keys and the mouse buttons are fixed.
- With units selected, hover a visible enemy and the *Projected fire* panel beside it shows each unit's hit chance — and what is degrading it — before you commit to the shot. Press `o` (rebindable) to open the debug overlay — while it is open, every hit, penetration and APS roll the model makes streams through its feed with the full factor breakdown.

The live build sends play telemetry to `/api/events` on the same origin: a random player id kept in `localStorage`, a per-load session id, and session, tutorial-step, heartbeat, mission start/objective/end, campaign-progress and brigade-account events (credits, unlocks, upgrades, purchases, unit and order counts). The game's own database keeps no e-mail, IP or URL — the connecting IP is used only in memory to rate-limit — but Cloudflare's persisted Workers Logs and traces keep each request URL with its query string, so a `?tester=` label lands there too. It is off in dev builds and on localhost unless `?telemetry` is on the URL, honours Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track (which win even over `?telemetry`), and `?notrack` opts out and persists that choice in `localStorage`; `?tester=<name>` labels a tester cohort. Aggregates are read on `/stats`, which is password-protected.

## What is in it

![The Free play task force on Beit Sahwan's outskirts — two Lavi tanks, two Namers, an Eitan, the D9, a jeep, a Peten gunship, infantry, an AT team, a mortar team and a recon drone, drawn as lit 3D meshes](docs/screenshots/units.png)

*Captured from the shipped three.js renderer on 2026-09-30 at 1440×900 (`/free-play/beit_sahwan_outskirts`, the force walked five tiles off its assembly point, zoom 1.6).*

**Combat.** Detection, six-factor hit resolution, penetration curves, component damage (mobility and firepower kills are separate outcomes), APS interception, suppression and rout. Cover is the big lever: `COVER_HIT` steps 1 / .375 / .1375 / .09 and `COVER_SUPP` 1 / .475 / .1375 / .09 — a dug-in target is both harder to hit and harder to pin, which is why clearing a held town takes roughly 3:1. Every knob the balance backtest calibrates against the GDD §5.7 targets lives in `packages/sim/src/tuning.ts`, mostly as raw Q16.16 integers with the decimal value in a trailing comment (tick counts, shifts and percent weights are plain integers), sectioned by GDD §5.1–5.6 followed by projectiles, veterancy, movement, smoke and the other ability knobs.

**Missions and campaign.** 27 mission files: 26 campaign missions plus the tutorial. Three fronts open in order on a 3D diorama of the Sahar Basin that you turn by drag, arrow keys or the rotate buttons (a flat map stands in on the Pixi renderer, or when WebGL2 or the board mesh is unavailable): the Marj Strip (Beit Sahwan 5, Khan Rafid 3, Deir Amun 3), Sur (Tel Marum 3, Qarn Hadid 3, Umm Zeitoun 4) and Naharin (Wadi Halam 5). The war opens with *First Light*, a dawn siege — what is left of a company holds a compound on the Beit Sahwan road until the relief column arrives, the one mission whose lead phase is `breach`; every other mission is led by one of the five remaining phases — recon, foothold, build-up, clearance or subterranean. Each town shows missions done and stars earned, each front carries a villain card, and the board keeps the account of the 19 taken, moved only by rescues. Objectives are declarative data, never TypeScript: the schema's vocabulary is twelve types — `locate`, `hold_for`, `survive_until`, `capture`, `eliminate_hvt`, `evacuate_before`, `collapse`, `raze`, `destroy_all`, plus `mark`, `escort` and `no_collateral_above`, which the schema accepts but the runtime rejects (`MissionRuntime` throws on them) and no mission uses. Every shipped mission declares `target_minutes` (all 27 do, 5–7 minutes, tutorial 10), and the schema caps the value: `minimum: 5`, `maximum: 7` via an `if/then/else` at the schema root whose one named exemption is `beit_sahwan_0_tutorial` at 10. The field itself is optional (`default: 6`). The sim never reads it and it is never graded; the app shows it on the post-mission debrief as "Time: {clock} of {target}:00", and the Worker's /stats page shows it as each mission's Target column. `pnpm playtest` proves each of the 26 campaign missions winnable by a scripted plan (the tutorial is a step machine driven by player input and has no plan).

**Conduct and progression.** Every mission scores Conduct 0–100 — civilian casualties, damage to flagged structures and disproportionate ordnance deduct — and each victory is graded ★ (won), ★★ (Conduct at or above the mission's floor + 20, or 70 where none is declared) or ★★★ (Ari'im citation: every carrying secondary complete — a mission with none caps at ★★, and only 7 of the 27 missions declare one). A ledger carries named units with a service record (150 on strength, the overflow in reserve), Conduct ratings, stars, intel, evacuated civilians and recovered hostages between missions; promotion runs Captain → Major → Lieutenant Colonel → Colonel. Units unlock on campaign-average Conduct (floors of 70–90 on nine units, `mbt_lavi` at 90), on total stars (12, 30 and 44 for `breach_team`, `scout_shachaf` and `apc_kipod`), or by paying a price in credits — the earned path is always free, and collateral damage costs you options rather than a lecture.

**Brigade.** A campaign win pays credits: 100 for the win, 40 per carrying secondary completed, 10 per starting-force unit brought home, 1 per Conduct point over the ★★ floor; a replay pays only the improvement, a defeat nothing. Spend them in the garage: open a gated unit early, or buy three-tier `armour`, `sensors` and `firepower` tracks that apply to every unit of that type — each track priced on its own, so `inf_squad` armour runs 115/175/250 while its firepower runs 145/220/305, and `mbt_lavi` armour runs 360/545/770 against 455/680/950 for firepower (the D9 and the recon drone carry armour and sensors only). Bought tiers are applied by a pure pre-pass (`applyUpgrades` in `@lions/data`, run by the app before `sim.addUnitType`), so the sim registers already-patched unit JSON and never learns a tier exists. Credits are priced separately by `creditsFor` in `@lions/sim`, which the simulation itself never calls — only the app and the playtest harness do. The account survives a new campaign. Credits are never sold; three bought-only special-forces units are designed and staged under `docs/campaign/special_units/e5/`, not shipped.

**Command.** Control groups with a group bar, attack-move that sweeps toward last contact instead of stalling, garrisoning, mount/dismount, smoke screens (carried by nine KDF types), demolition, field production and reinforcement from a dock, and intel sinks — a satellite sweep and a precision strike. Around the fight: an alert feed, an objectives tracker, a 210×210 minimap that shows the whole map but only the enemies you are currently observing, a pause menu with settings, save slots with export and import, a deploy screen that picks which veterans fill a mission's slots, and a debrief with Conduct deductions by reason, losses by name and the credit payout.

**Presentation.** A lit three.js renderer since v0.63.0 (2026-09-15) — one directional sun with a map-wide shadow map, a hemisphere sky/ground bounce, ACES tone mapping with sRGB output, and a post chain with ambient occlusion (GTAO) and SMAA antialiasing; since v0.70.0 (2026-09-18) the video-quality preset gates it — `high` (the default) keeps AO, SMAA and a 4096 shadow map, `medium` drops AO, `low` drops both and halves the shadow map again. It draws rigged meshes for every unit, vehicle and building: 89 GLBs, Draco-encoded to 27.04 MiB in `assets/meshes/`, loaded per mission roster. Infantry crossfade between clips (code-authored in `rig.py` for the kit-built teams, supplied for the Meshy ones) and die on screen — three teams play an authored fall, the rest topple figure by figure into a prone wreck pose; vehicles pitch, roll and settle under motion and leave a persistent 3D wreck. The ground is a smooth surface over tile centres (the sim reads only the integer grid), splat-blended, with roads that read as tracks, a decal pool, clustered scatter, props, trees that sway, dust haze and dawn/day/dusk/night presets a mission can set (`night` currently lights as dusk). Mortar bombs and rockets arc, tank and autocannon rounds fly as bolts, the Spike climbs and dives onto the roof while other guided missiles weave in low, and every vehicle kill has a blast with a real light, screen shake and hit-stop. Colour is still authored from `data/palette.json` — 41 ramp colours plus runtime-only VFX, team and control-group bands (18 colours) and the team band's colour-vision-deficiency variants, 71 declared slots in all — as the input to lighting, but the per-pixel palette guarantee was retired on this backend on 2026-09-14. Sprite sheets still ship too — 43 sheets, 3,787 PNGs, 63.4 MiB in `assets/sprites/` — and not only for `?renderer=pixi` and `&nomesh`: the default renderer still draws the six unit types with no GLB (`gun_truck`, the three drones, `manpad_team`, `recoilless_team`) from their sheets, falls back to a sheet wreck for a vehicle whose GLB has no `wreck` clip, and billboards a just-built unit until its mesh lands. The simulation never sees any of it (Architecture, invariant 4).

**Audio.** Music, SFX sets and voice lines are declared in `data/audio.json` and gated by `pnpm validate:audio`. A voice director picks one line per gesture from the cursor's winning verb and degrades repeats line → acknowledgement → silence; the brigade speaks Hebrew, Ashwar, Sarim and Rif speak Arabic, civilians are silent. Ten Hebrew voice files ship so far — five recordings covering four of the manifest's twenty Hebrew lines (ack, move, attack, and two takes of death), each in ogg and m4a — and no Arabic ones; a line with no recording plays nothing and is noted once per key in dev builds, never raised as an error. Every unplaced voice line — an order acknowledged, a KDF death called in, the radio in the player's ear — passes through a 300–3400 Hz radio band; enemy death lines are placed in the world instead and skip it. The walkie-talkie effect (drive, compression, key-up click, squelch, noise bed) is on by default and applied at playback, so the files stay clean. A captions setting prints the line's English text above the order row.

## Status

The combat model is the product, and it is calibrated. `pnpm balance` runs six cases twice — on the shipped roster and again with every KDF unit at its maximum upgrade tiers — and both tables must pass; it exits 1 on any miss, takes roughly 40–50 s on Apple silicon (two runs on 2026-09-30 printed 45.4 s and 48.6 s; the figure moves with machine load) and runs in CI on every PR and every push to main. Four cases are the GDD §5.7 targets; the air case is the harness's own addition, and the smoke step is a separate GDD-specified case (lead ruling D1, 2026-09-29) that §5.7 deliberately keeps outside the four model targets. The GDD's three extended targets (ambush, standoff, raid) are not measured by anything yet.

| Backtest case | Base roster | Max upgrade tier |
|---|---|---|
| Urban assault needs ≈3:1 attacker:defender (60 seeds per ratio) | 1:1 = 0% · 2:1 = 63% · **3:1 = 100%** · 4:1 = 100% | 1:1 = 0% · 2:1 = 55% · **3:1 = 80%** · 4:1 = 98% |
| Smoke buys exactly one ratio step (one screen per assault group) | 1:1+smoke = 0% · **2:1+smoke = 100%** (60/60) | 1:1+smoke = 0% · **2:1+smoke = 97%** (58/60) |
| ATGM Pk vs unprotected armour ≈ 0.7 (0.60–0.80) | **0.67** over 400 launches | **0.77** over 400 launches |
| APS intercept 0.6–0.9 vs shaped charge | **0.73** over 400 engagements | **0.73** over 400 engagements |
| Lanchester's square law emerges (20 seeds per case) | 12v6 → 12.0 survivors (square 10.4, linear 6) · 16v8 → 16.0 (square 13.9, linear 8) | same |
| Air is contested by AA (harness target, not in GDD §5.7; 30 seeds per case) | 1 gun truck → 80% survival · 2 → 0% · 3 → **0%** | 1 → 100% · 2 → 30% · 3 → **30%** |

Before group formations landed (2026-09-15) the urban 2:1 read 15%, and that was a stacking artifact — every group member sent to one tile inside the near-miss suppression radius — not a property of the town. Where the GDD's formula text and its §5.7 targets disagree, a passing backtest wins.

Done: the calibrated combat model (M0) and the Beit Sahwan arc with its campaign ledger and Conduct scoring, and six more town arcs followed — 26 campaign missions in seven towns, plus a tutorial, as of v0.94.0. The shell upgrade has landed its first three phases — a router, settings, a pause menu, save slots, an alert feed, an objectives tracker, the minimap and the garage — and unit voices arrived beside it as their own audio work; Phase 3's remaining half (deploy as a decision, victory and defeat moments, and the art pass over garage plates, portraits, key art and the campaign board) is in flight, and Phase 4 (Steam Deck, controller) is unscheduled. The programme now runs toward the commander's HUD and one visual register, due 30 October 2026 (the ledger's M1; then 27 November, 12 February and 16 April 2027). The live ledger is [`docs/HANDOVER.md`](docs/HANDOVER.md).

## Quickstart

Node 22 (what CI runs; the repo does not pin Node, and the lead develops on Node 25 — the one known difference is vitest's jsdom `localStorage`) and pnpm 11.17.0 (the `packageManager` pin in `package.json`). The sprite gate needs Python 3 with `numpy` and `pillow`, and the audio gate needs Python 3 with `numpy`; the mesh gate needs both of those plus Blender (CI installs 5.2.0), found through `BLENDER_BIN`, `blender` on `PATH`, the macOS `/Applications/Blender.app`, or `--blender`; the visual gate and the UI tools need Playwright Chromium.

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm dev          # Vite on http://localhost:5173 — the main menu; /free-play/<map id> (or ?sandbox=<map id>) walks a map without a mission
```

On any battlefield — a mission or Free play — press `o` to open the debug overlay: every hit, penetration and APS roll the model makes while it is open streams through it — a status pane on the left, the roll feed on the right; it is off by default. In the console, `__lions.help()` (also printed automatically on every Free play boot) prints the map that loaded, which flags are on, the Free play extras (`&roe`, `&tunnel`, `&sur`, `&civ`, `&ditch`, `&nomesh`, `&decals`, `&kit`, plus `&tod=dawn|day|dusk|night`), every shipped map id, and the console helpers it lists — `step(n)`, `goto(marker)`, `units()`, `sel([id])`, `cursorKey()`, `voiceLog()`; `hover(x, y)` is on `__lions` too but is not in the printed line. Other URL parameters the build reads (`renderer`, `lang`, `nosw`, `pseudo`, `voicetick`, `fresh`, `tutorial`) are not printed by help. `__lions` exists only on a battlefield; the menu defines none.

Three headless walkers — `walk_mission.ts`, `walk_placements.ts` and `walk_carryover.ts` under `tools/src/` — load an authored mission the way the app does and print what it actually does at chosen times, which is how content bugs that pass every fixture-based test (a trigger naming an undeclared group, a drop timed after the carrier has driven past) get found. None is a pnpm script, and `tsx` is a devDependency of the tools workspace only, so each runs through it:

```bash
pnpm --filter @lions/tools exec tsx src/walk_mission.ts beit_sahwan_breach 0 60 150 300   # the real mission's world at chosen seconds
pnpm --filter @lions/tools exec tsx src/walk_placements.ts wadi_halam_5_depot              # the map with every placement overlaid; fails on a bad one
pnpm --filter @lions/tools exec tsx src/walk_carryover.ts                                  # Beit Sahwan I's recon intel (intel.marked_positions) carried into III: fresh vs carried spawns side by side, fails on a tag the two missions disagree on
```

The first is the tool that catches a trigger timed past the end of a mission, or a group nothing commits.

## Architecture

pnpm workspace with a one-way dependency direction: `app → render → sim`, with `data` a leaf that `app`, `tools` and `worker` also import; `tools` sits alongside (it may import `sim` and `data` only), and `packages/worker` — the Cloudflare Worker that serves the build and the telemetry endpoints (`/api/events` into D1, `/stats`) — imports only `data`. Lint enforces the direction for `sim`, `render`, `data` and `tools`. The renderer sits behind one interface (`packages/render/src/api.ts`), and both backends arrive by dynamic import, so a player downloads only the one they run.

Four load-bearing invariants (see `CLAUDE.md`):

1. The sim runs a fixed 20 Hz tick; the renderer interpolates between ticks on every frame.
2. `@lions/sim` is Q16.16 fixed-point (`fixed.ts`, `ONE = 65536`). Lint bans `Math`, `Date`, `parseFloat`, wall-clock timers, `crypto` and float literals in `packages/sim/src` (test files exempt, so they can use `Math` as an oracle); `pnpm test:determinism` pins the replay hash. Trig/exp/Φ come from committed lookup tables (`packages/sim/src/gen/tables.ts`); `pnpm gen:luts` regenerates them, and the sim reads the committed integers rather than computing them, so another engine's `Math.*` cannot leak into the sim at all. Only the committed file is the source of truth: regenerating on a different engine can move an entry by 1 ulp, and the determinism hash catches that only where the replay reads that entry, so a regenerated table must be diffed, not trusted.
3. All randomness is a seeded per-entity PRNG — entity counts can change mid-mission without reshuffling anyone's rolls.
4. Commands in → sim → state + events out. Nothing outside the sim mutates sim state.

Determinism is not aspirational: `pnpm test:determinism` replays 1000 ticks from seed `0x1310_0001` twice and asserts the golden hash `2109596329`, and CI runs it on Linux, macOS and Windows. Content is JSON validated against `data/schemas/`; a new unit is JSON plus art — a mesh, and a sprite sheet for the billboard path — and the sim never changes for it, though the app's asset tables (`mesh-catalogue.ts`, `SPRITE_MAP`) still need an entry.

## Contributing

Read `CONTRIBUTING.md` first — units, missions, VFX and maps are JSON gated by validators (missions target 5–7 minutes and the schema is the authority, where `CONTRIBUTING.md` still says 12–20), and `python tools/validate_balance.py` holds every non-civilian unit to within ±18% of the fitted cost curve. Sprites go through the locked render rig (`tools/render_rig.py`) and `pnpm validate:assets`, which enforces exact palette match, binary alpha, framing, minimum fill and a pairwise silhouette-readability check (IoU under 0.88 at gameplay zoom). Meshes go through `pnpm validate:meshes`, which renders every unit, vehicle and building GLB headlessly and reuses those same checks — except that thirteen named textured buildings and vehicles are exempt from the palette, framing and fill checks and keep only the silhouette check, and decor, props and VFX meshes are checked against the mesh contract alone. A mesh style bible (`docs/art/style-bible.md`) is drafted and awaiting the lead's approval; it is guidance for new meshes, not a gate. AI-generated art is permitted, including for shipping assets, provided the PR discloses it. `docs/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md` is a dated snapshot of where shipped assets came from and which cannot ship as-is — not a gate, and by its own account still missing an origin for 33 meshes. Contributions are accepted under `CLA.md` with a DCO sign-off (`git commit -s`).
